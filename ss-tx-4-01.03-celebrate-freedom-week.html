Word Roots
Latin Root: liber — "free"

The Latin root liber means "free." The founders used words built from this root again and again because freedom was at the heart of everything they were building.

📖

liberty — the state of being free · liberal — generous or open-minded · liberate — to set free · library — originally a "free" collection of books

Discovery Fact
The word "library" comes from the Latin "liber," which originally meant the inner bark of a tree — because that's what ancient people wrote on before paper was invented! Over time, "liber" came to mean "book" and then "a collection of books."

Three Documents That Built a Nation
The Declaration, the Constitution, and the Bill of Rights

The Declaration of Independence (1776) explains WHY the American colonies separated from Britain. It says that all people have rights that cannot be taken away — life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. Government gets its power from the people, not the other way around.

The U.S. Constitution (1787) creates the plan for HOW the government works. The Preamble begins: "We the People" — showing that the government belongs to the citizens. It sets up three branches so no one person or group has too much power.

The Bill of Rights (1791) is the first ten amendments to the Constitution. These protect individual freedoms — like freedom of speech, freedom of religion, and the right to a fair trial. Without the Bill of Rights, the government could take away people's freedoms.

🤔

Pause & Think: Why do you think the founders wrote these ideas down instead of just talking about them? What's different about putting important ideas in writing?

What do I see? The Declaration states that people are "created equal" with "unalienable Rights" — rights that cannot be taken away. It says government must have the "consent of the governed."

💭

What does it mean? "Consent of the governed" means the government's power comes from the people who agree to be governed. If the government stops protecting rights, the people can change it.

⚖️

Why does it matter? These words created the foundation for American self-government. Every right you have today — freedom of speech, freedom of religion, the right to a fair trial — traces back to these founding ideas.

Key Concept

The Declaration of Independence explains why people have the right to govern themselves. The Constitution creates a plan for that government. The Bill of Rights protects individual freedoms. Together, these three documents are the foundation of American self-governance.

1. What does the Declaration of Independence explain?
AHow the government is organized into three branches
BWhy the colonies had the right to separate from Britain
CWhich freedoms are protected for individuals
That's right! The Declaration explains WHY the colonies separated — because people have unalienable rights and government must have the consent of the governed.
Think about the purpose of the Declaration. The Constitution organizes government, and the Bill of Rights protects freedoms. The Declaration explains why the colonies had the right to be free.
2. What does "consent of the governed" mean?
AThe government gets its power from the people
BThe king decides what rights people have
COnly government leaders can make rules
Exactly! "Consent of the governed" means the people agree to be governed — and the government's power comes from them, not the other way around.
Remember: "consent" means agreement, and "governed" means the people. So "consent of the governed" means the people agree to give the government its power.
3. Why is the Bill of Rights important?
AIt explains why the colonies left Britain
BIt creates the three branches of government
CIt protects individual freedoms from the government
Correct! The Bill of Rights protects individual freedoms — like speech, religion, and a fair trial — so the government cannot take them away.
The Declaration explains the separation, and the Constitution creates the branches. The Bill of Rights specifically protects individual freedoms from the government.
