CANVAS QUIZ: Unit 12 Assessment — Texas Through Time Capstone
Course: Grade 4 Texas Social Studies
Standards: Priority review 4.1–4.18 · 4.19A–G · 4.20A–B · 4.21A–E · 4.22A–B
Points per question: 1
Question count: 15 (12 multiple choice + 3 short answer)
Note to teacher: The capstone exhibit and presentation (Lessons 12.01–12.04) are the main performance grade. This quiz checks year-end knowledge and the research skills used in the capstone.

---

MULTIPLE CHOICE

1. Which list puts these events in the correct order, from first to last?
a) Texas Revolution → Spanish missions → Spindletop
*b) Spanish missions → Texas Revolution → Spindletop
c) Spindletop → Spanish missions → Texas Revolution
d) Texas Revolution → Spindletop → Spanish missions

2. In what year did Texas become a state of the United States?
a) 1821
b) 1836
*c) 1845
d) 1876

3. What happened in Galveston on June 19, 1865?
a) The Lucas Gusher blew in.
*b) Freedom was announced to enslaved people in Texas (Juneteenth).
c) Texas declared independence from Mexico.
d) The Texas Capitol was finished.

4. "Ranching has been important in Texas from the Spanish missions to today." This statement describes...
a) change
*b) continuity
c) a bibliography
d) supply

5. "Texas went from part of Mexico, to an independent republic, to a U.S. state." This statement describes...
*a) change
b) continuity
c) opportunity cost
d) a caption

6. Which is the strongest caption for an exhibit?
a) "Oil."
b) "A picture I liked."
*c) "The Lucas Gusher, Spindletop, 1901. It began the Texas oil boom, which changed where Texans worked."
d) "Something happened near Beaumont."

7. What does a bibliography tell a visitor?
a) The curator's favorite unit
*b) Where each source came from
c) The museum's opening hours
d) The answer to every question

8. Which test asks, "Does this evidence help answer MY question?"
*a) Relevant
b) Reliable
c) Specific
d) Colorful

9. Which source is a PRIMARY source?
a) A website summary written this year
*b) The 1883 railroad map printed by the Galveston News
c) Your textbook chapter about railroads
d) A classmate's exhibit caption

10. A visitor asks a question you cannot fully answer. What is the most honest response?
a) Make up an answer.
b) Ignore the question.
*c) Explain what your evidence shows and what you would still need to find out.
d) Change the subject.

11. Which shows courteous peer feedback?
a) "This is bad."
*b) "I like how you used three units. One idea to make it stronger is to add a date to your second caption."
c) Saying nothing and walking away
d) "Mine is better."

12. Which of these is one of the seven pillars studied this year?
a) Luck
b) Speed
*c) Self-governance
d) Popularity

SHORT ANSWER

13. Write a claim about one pattern of change or continuity in Texas history. Support it with two pieces of evidence from two different units.
[Rubric: 2 pts — States a clear claim about change or continuity AND supports it with two accurate pieces of evidence from two different units (e.g., transportation changed from cattle trails to railroads to ports; Juneteenth celebrated from 1866 to today). 1 pt — Claim with one accurate piece of evidence, or two pieces of evidence without a clear claim. 0 pts — No accurate claim or evidence.]

14. Name one part of the Texas story that must never be left out. Explain why, including whose experience it helps us understand.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Names an accurate event or group (e.g., American Indian nations, Juneteenth, the Red River War, Tejano contributions) AND explains why it matters, naming the people affected. 1 pt — Names an accurate event with a vague reason. 0 pts — Inaccurate or no response.]

15. What does the Texas story ask a responsible citizen to remember, question, and carry forward? Give one example for each.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Gives an accurate, thoughtful example for all three (remember: a person, event, or group; question: whose voice is missing / what the evidence shows; carry forward: learn, speak, serve, vote, preserve). 1 pt — Examples for two of the three. 0 pts — One or none.]
