CANVAS QUIZ: Unit 10 Assessment — Modern Texas: Culture, Science, and Technology
Course: Grade 4 Texas Social Studies
Standards: 4.11D · 4.14D · 4.17A–B · 4.18A–B
Points per question: 1
Question count: 15 (12 multiple choice + 3 short answer)

---

MULTIPLE CHOICE

1. What is culture?
a) Only the food a group eats
*b) The shared way of life of a group, including its music, art, food, language, and celebrations
c) A kind of government
d) A map of a region

2. Lydia Mendoza was known as "La Alondra de la Frontera." What does that mean?
a) The Queen of the Boleros
*b) The Lark of the Border
c) The Voice of Waco
d) The Star of Houston

3. Which singer from Brownsville was called "the Queen of the Boleros"?
a) Lydia Mendoza
*b) Chelo Silva
c) Jules Bledsoe
d) Bessie Coleman

4. Jules Bledsoe of Waco is best known for...
a) inventing condensed milk.
*b) being the first to sing "Ol' Man River" in the Broadway musical Show Boat in 1927.
c) flying on the space shuttle.
d) starting Charro Days.

5. Which sentence DESCRIBES a photograph instead of interpreting it?
a) She was proud of her music.
b) Her fans loved her.
*c) She is holding a large guitar.
d) She was the best singer in Texas.

6. Conjunto music mixes Mexican songs with an instrument brought to Texas by German, Czech, and Polish immigrants. Which instrument?
a) The violin
b) The drum
*c) The accordion
d) The piano

7. A strong museum label should include...
a) a joke about the artist.
*b) the name and date, a detail, the community, and why it matters.
c) only the artist's favorite color.
d) as many exclamation points as possible.

8. What problem did Gail Borden's condensed milk solve?
*a) Fresh milk spoiled quickly, especially on long trips.
b) Cattle wandered onto farms.
c) Astronauts lost bone strength.
d) Surgeons could not keep blood moving.

9. Why was barbed wire important on the Texas Plains?
a) It kept milk fresh.
*b) There was little wood for fences, and barbed wire was cheap and strong.
c) It helped heart surgeons.
d) It carried messages like a telegraph.

10. Michael DeBakey created his roller pump in 1932. About 20 years later, it became part of the heart-lung machine. What does this show?
a) Inventions always matter the day they are created.
*b) An invention may become historically important when it is adopted and changes lives, not only when it is created.
c) Inventions never change anything.
d) Patents are not real documents.

11. Millie Hughes-Fulford, born in Mineral Wells, Texas, flew on the space shuttle Columbia in 1991 to study...
a) how to build barbed wire in space
*b) how the human body, including bones, changes in space
c) how to make condensed milk
d) how music sounds in space

12. Which is a NEW CONSEQUENCE of barbed wire in Texas?
a) Milk stayed fresh longer.
*b) Fences closed the open range and led to fence-cutting fights in 1883.
c) Open-heart surgery became possible.
d) Astronauts could sleep in space.

SHORT ANSWER

13. Choose Lydia Mendoza, Chelo Silva, or Jules Bledsoe. Write a short museum label that names the artist, gives one detail, names the community, and explains why the artist matters.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Label includes an accurate name/place or date, a specific detail, the community (e.g., Mexican American communities of Texas and the border; African American community of Waco), AND why the artist matters (preserved a tradition and/or carried it to new audiences). 1 pt — Accurate but missing the community or the "why it matters." 0 pts — Inaccurate or no label.]

14. How can art and tradition preserve a community's story while also changing over time? Use one example from this unit.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Explains BOTH preserving (keeping songs, language, festivals, or styles alive) AND changing (new audiences, records, radio, Broadway, mixing traditions like conjunto) with an accurate example. 1 pt — Explains only preserving or only changing, with an example. 0 pts — No accurate explanation.]

15. Choose one innovator: Gail Borden, Joseph Glidden, Michael DeBakey, or Millie Hughes-Fulford. Build a short impact chain: the problem, the idea, one benefit (for individuals, businesses, or society), and one new consequence or later change.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Accurately names the problem, the innovation, a benefit, AND a consequence or later change. 1 pt — Names the problem and innovation accurately but is missing the benefit or consequence. 0 pts — Inaccurate or incomplete.]
