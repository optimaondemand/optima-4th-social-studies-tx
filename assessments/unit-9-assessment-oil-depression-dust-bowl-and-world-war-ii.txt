CANVAS QUIZ: Unit 9 Assessment — Oil, Depression, Dust Bowl, and World War II
Course: Grade 4 Texas Social Studies
Standards: 4.5A,B · 4.7B · 4.8C · 4.11A–D · 4.19A–G
Points per question: 1
Question count: 15 (12 multiple choice + 3 short answer)

---

MULTIPLE CHOICE

1. What happened at Spindletop, near Beaumont, on January 10, 1901?
a) The first railroad reached Texas.
*b) A huge oil gusher blew in and started the Texas oil boom.
c) A dust storm buried the town.
d) Texas joined the United States.

2. How did Pattillo Higgins show perseverance?
a) He gave up when experts said he was wrong.
*b) He kept believing there was oil under Spindletop Hill and searched for a driller after his wells failed.
c) He moved to California to find gold.
d) He built the first refinery in Port Arthur.

3. Texas produced about 836,000 barrels of oil in 1900 and more than 18 million barrels in 1902. What does this data show?
a) Oil production went down after Spindletop.
*b) Oil production grew more than 20 times bigger in two years.
c) Texas stopped producing oil.
d) Oil production stayed the same.

4. Spindletop's own wells ran low by 1904. Why do historians still call Spindletop a turning point?
a) Because the gusher photo was famous
*b) Because the oil industry it started kept growing across Texas for decades
c) Because the hill grew taller
d) Because it ended the Great Depression

5. Which sentence describes the LARGER oil boom, not just Pattillo Higgins?
a) He formed the Gladys City company in 1892.
b) He placed ads looking for someone who could drill.
*c) Hundreds of companies, refineries, and pipelines grew in Beaumont, Port Arthur, and Houston.
d) He noticed gas bubbling on Spindletop Hill.

6. Which is an ECONOMIC cause of hard times in the 1930s?
a) Drought
b) Strong winds
*c) Cotton prices falling to about 5 cents a pound
d) Bare, dry soil

7. Why did soil blow away in the Texas Panhandle during the Dust Bowl?
*a) Native grass had been plowed up, and drought dried out the bare soil.
b) Too much rain fell on the fields.
c) Oil wells pushed the soil into the air.
d) Cattle ate all the soil.

8. How did low crop prices make the Dust Bowl worse?
a) Farmers stopped farming completely, so grass grew back.
*b) Farmers plowed even more land to make up money, leaving more bare soil.
c) Low prices caused more rain.
d) Low prices had no effect on the land.

9. Dorothea Lange's 1937 photo of former Texas tenant farmers added what new cause to the crisis model?
a) Earthquakes
b) Floods
*c) Tractors ("power farming") replaced tenant farm families.
d) Oil spills

10. Which helped Texans recover from the Dust Bowl?
a) Plowing up more grassland
*b) Contour plowing, shelterbelts, and Civilian Conservation Corps jobs
c) Leaving fields bare
d) Burning crops

11. What was the "home front" during World War II?
a) A battle fought in Texas
*b) The people and work at home that supported the war
c) The front porch of the Alamo
d) A type of airplane

12. Which is ZOOM-OUT evidence about the whole state?
a) Cleto Rodríguez sold newspapers as a boy.
b) Audie Murphy grew up in a sharecropping family.
*c) More than 750,000 Texans served in uniform during World War II.
d) Bessie Coleman learned French.

---

SHORT ANSWER

13. What evidence proves that Spindletop was a turning point rather than only a dramatic event? Use at least one number and one lasting change.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Gives an accurate number (e.g., oil production 836,000 barrels in 1900 to more than 18 million in 1902; Beaumont about 9,000 to 20,640 by 1910) AND a lasting change (e.g., new oil fields across Texas, refineries and pipelines, companies like Gulf Oil and the Texas Company, city growth, Texas leading the nation in oil by 1928). 1 pt — Gives a number OR a lasting change, but not both. 0 pts — No accurate evidence.]

14. Explain how one environmental problem and one economic problem made each other worse in Texas during the 1930s. Then name one way Texans showed perseverance.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Accurately connects an environmental problem (drought, wind, bare soil, dust storms) to an economic problem (low prices, debt, lost jobs, tractors replacing tenants) in a cause-and-effect chain AND names an accurate example of perseverance or recovery (most families stayed, gardens and sharing, contour plowing, shelterbelts, CCC jobs). 1 pt — Names both problems without explaining the connection, or explains the connection without a perseverance example. 0 pts — Inaccurate or no explanation.]

15. Choose one Texan: Bessie Coleman, Audie Murphy, or Cleto Rodríguez. Explain how that person showed service, and connect their story to a larger change in 20th-century Texas. Do not let one person replace the larger story.
[Rubric: 2 pts — Accurately describes the person's service (e.g., Coleman earned her pilot's license in France in 1921 when U.S. schools shut her out; Murphy and Rodríguez received the Medal of Honor and later helped others / spoke about veterans) AND connects it to a larger change with statewide or national evidence (e.g., 750,000 Texans in uniform, new factory jobs, opportunities for Black pilots like the Tuskegee Airmen). 1 pt — Describes the person accurately but gives no larger connection, or treats one person as the whole story. 0 pts — Inaccurate or no response.]
