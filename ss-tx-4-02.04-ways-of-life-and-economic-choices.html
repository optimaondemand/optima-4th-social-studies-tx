Trade and Choice: Comparing Ways of Life
Build a comparison matrix of four Indigenous groups

Today you'll build a comparison matrix examining how four Indigenous groups — the Lipan Apache, Karankawa, Caddo, and Jumano — met their needs. You'll compare them across five categories: food source, shelter type, movement pattern, trade activity, and geographic region.

Your matrix will show how geography shaped different ways of life. When you're done, you'll write a conclusion explaining the geographic reason behind one pattern you notice.

Now that you've met the key ideas, let's examine the evidence up close.

Trade and Choice: Group by Group
Comparing needs and resources across groups

Your comparison matrix has four columns — one for each group: Lipan Apache, Karankawa, Caddo, and Jumano. The rows are: food source, shelter type, movement pattern, trade activity, and geographic region. Start by filling in what you know from the lesson.

The Lipan Apache lived on the plains, hunted bison, built portable tipis, and moved with the herds. The Karankawa lived along the Gulf coast, fished and gathered shellfish, built portable shelters, and moved seasonally along the coast.

The Caddo lived in the piney woods of East Texas, farmed corn and other crops, built permanent grass houses, and stayed in one place. The Jumano lived in the arid Trans-Pecos region, farmed near rivers and traded widely, and built adobe structures.

After filling in your matrix, look for patterns. Which groups were nomadic? Which were sedentary? How did geography determine whether a group farmed or hunted? Use these patterns to write your conclusion.

Connect the Ideas
Key Concept

The Lipan Apache, Karankawa, Caddo, and Jumano each met their needs differently because they lived in different geographic regions. Nomadic groups followed resources; sedentary groups farmed where the land was rich. Geography shaped every choice.

🦉
Opti says...
The word "commercium" means "trade together." When groups traded, they connected across geographic boundaries. Trade shows that even groups with very different ways of life found reasons to cooperate.

Quick Self-Check
🧠

Not graded — just for you! Answer these three questions to see how much you remember before writing your evidence responses.

1. Why did the Lipan Apache move frequently while the Caddo stayed in one place?
AThe Lipan Apache enjoyed traveling more than the Caddo
BThe Lipan Apache followed bison herds on the plains, while the Caddo farmed fertile land in East Texas
CThe Caddo were not allowed to travel
That's right! Geography shaped movement patterns. The plains provided bison but required following the herds, while East Texas soil supported farming in one place.
Think about what each group's geography provided. How did the land determine whether they moved or stayed?
2. What does the word "barter" mean?
ATo give things away for free
BTo steal from another group
CTo trade goods directly without using money
That's right! Barter means trading goods directly — like exchanging bison hides for corn — without using money.
Think about how groups exchanged goods before money existed. What did they do instead?
3. Which group is correctly matched with its way of life?
AJumano — farmed near rivers and traded widely across regions
BKarankawa — farmed corn in permanent villages
CCaddo — followed bison herds across the plains
Correct! The Jumano lived in the arid Trans-Pecos region, farmed along rivers where water was available, and were known as long-distance traders.
Think carefully about each group's geographic region. The Karankawa lived along the coast and the Caddo lived in the piney woods — does that match farming or bison hunting?
